# Project 104 — GQP Geodesic Quantum Computing Validation Lab MAX

## Status
PROPOSED → ARCHITECTURE BASELINE → PROJECT GENESIS 2026-09-10

## Mission
Create a falsifiable research and engineering laboratory for the supplied **Geodesic Quantum Processor (GQP)** concept. The project does not assume that the proposed TRS/GQP claims are physically or computationally correct. Its purpose is to translate those claims into formal definitions, numerical experiments, hardware-test specifications, baselines and reproducible evidence.

- **P77** owns general mathematical formalization and proof-status discipline.
- **P79** owns autonomous scientific discovery across broad domains.
- **P80** owns the self-evolving computing substrate.
- **P61** owns OmniCore convergence and heterogeneous runtime architecture.
- **P104** owns the concrete **GQP research program**: geodesic-computation hypotheses, quantum-hardware mapping, WGS84/GIS benchmark definitions, cryogenic/RF test plans, resource budgets and falsification gates.

The two supplied GQP documents are overlapping witnesses and are treated as one project lineage, not two projects.

## Research tracks

### A. Mathematical validation
Formalize the proposed manifold, metric, Hamiltonian, geodesic objective and any TRS identities. Every statement receives an explicit status: theorem, derivation, numerical observation, conjecture or unsupported assertion.

### B. Algorithmic baseline
Compare any GQP/geodesic algorithm against classical geodesic solvers, graph optimization, GIS routing and appropriate quantum-algorithm baselines. Complexity claims must be stated in standard computational terms and cannot be inferred from geometric language alone.

### C. Quantum resource analysis
Before fabrication, estimate qubit count, connectivity, gate depth, error budget, coherence requirements, measurement overhead, reset strategy and classical-control requirements. A proposed topology is not evidence of a physical advantage.

### D. Hardware validation
The supplied grant material describes a possible path through isotopically enriched silicon, superconducting structures, RFSoC/AWG control, cryogenic operation, readout and a staged increase from small test chips toward larger topologies. P104 converts these into testable requirements rather than assuming successful implementation.

### E. Geodesy/GIS benchmark
Use WGS84/geodesic datasets and controlled synthetic problems with known solutions. Evaluate accuracy, runtime, energy, robustness to noise and scaling. Navigation claims require independent sensor and positioning benchmarks.

## Hard falsification gates
1. Undefined mathematical objects block promotion.
2. A claimed complexity advantage requires a formally specified computational problem and baseline.
3. Simulation cannot be presented as hardware evidence.
4. A single successful experiment is insufficient for a strong physical claim; replication is required.
5. Claimed navigation precision requires calibrated independent ground truth.
6. Energy advantage requires whole-system measurement, not only logical-operation estimates.
7. Any claimed quantum advantage requires statistically defensible comparison against classical baselines.
8. Grant figures remain estimates until procurement and accounting evidence exists.

## Safety and epistemic boundary
P104 is a research laboratory. It does not claim that GQP currently exists, has quantum advantage, defeats NP-complete complexity barriers, provides centimetre-scale GNSS-independent navigation, or realizes the proposed TRS physics. Such statements remain source-derived hypotheses until the verification program establishes them.
