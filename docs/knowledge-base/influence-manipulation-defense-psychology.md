# Influence and Manipulation: Defensive Psychology Layer

## Source basis

Derived from Anna Grzywa, *Manipulacja — mechanizmy psychologiczne* (1997), together with the supplied material on narrative/semantic influence.

The source describes manipulation as intentional, concealed influence that can reduce a person's freedom of decision, and it catalogs mechanisms involving self-esteem, ingratiation, cognitive dissonance, thinking biases, group pressure, authority, mechanical behavior, psychological resistance, guilt, Machiavellianism, text manipulation and group manipulation.

## Defensive use only

This material is archived as a detection and resilience taxonomy. It is **not** converted into instructions for covert persuasion, exploitation, dependency creation or vulnerability targeting.

The source emphasizes that manipulation can be planned and hidden and that multiple methods can be combined or sequenced.

## Defensive taxonomy

### 1. Self-esteem targeting

Detect communication that selectively inflates or attacks self-worth in order to shift a decision. The system should separate genuine feedback from instrumental status manipulation.

### 2. Ingratiation

Detect excessive flattery, approval-seeking pressure and status-based influence. The source discusses how perceived attractiveness or social approval can reduce critical evaluation.

### 3. Cognitive dissonance / consistency pressure

The source identifies the tendency to maintain consistency between prior choices, attitudes and behavior as a manipulation surface.

Defensive control:

- expose prior commitments when relevant;
- permit reconsideration without penalty;
- avoid framing consistency as a moral obligation;
- distinguish evidence from commitment pressure.

### 4. Thinking biases

Flag selective framing, premature closure and other reasoning shortcuts as possible influence surfaces. Detection must not itself claim that a user is irrational or compromised.

### 5. Group pressure

The source includes group pressure as a distinct manipulation mechanism and connects large-scale group influence with propaganda.

Defensive control:

- show whether a claim is based on consensus or evidence;
- expose missing alternatives;
- avoid artificial popularity counters;
- preserve the user's ability to disagree.

### 6. Authority cues

The source identifies invoking authorities and quotations as a manipulation surface.

Defensive control:

`authority claim -> source verification -> relevance check -> evidence status`

An authority label never substitutes for evidence.

### 7. Psychological reactance

The source describes the human sense of freedom and autonomous decision-making as a persistent psychological characteristic that can itself become a manipulation surface.

Defensive design therefore avoids coercive reverse-psychology patterns and preserves explicit choice.

### 8. Guilt / fear / pressure

Detect attempts to convert disagreement into moral condemnation, fear or obligation. The appropriate system response is disclosure and autonomy preservation, not counter-manipulation.

### 9. Text manipulation

The source explicitly discusses disinformation as a strategic form of manipulation and identifies text manipulation as a separate mechanism.

Defensive pipeline:

`claim -> provenance -> semantic comparison -> omitted-context check -> contradiction search -> confidence`

## Semantic manipulation

The supplied linguistic article demonstrates a related failure mode: a specialist term can be moved into public discourse and assigned a different, rhetorically loaded meaning. It describes this mechanism in relation to the Polish lexical family around "seksualizacja".

OmniCore should therefore distinguish:

- dictionary/technical meaning;
- contextual meaning;
- speaker-defined meaning;
- rhetorical redefinition;
- unsupported semantic loading.

## Influence-risk model

A defensive evaluator can represent a message as:

`InfluenceRisk = f(hidden_intent, pressure, authority, framing, omission, repetition, commitment_lock, personalization, reversibility)`

This is a detection score, not a diagnosis of the speaker or recipient.

## Human-agency gate

For recommendations or automated decisions:

1. disclose relevant uncertainty;
2. provide meaningful alternatives;
3. preserve opt-out;
4. avoid coercive urgency;
5. keep the decision reversible where feasible;
6. distinguish facts from interpretation;
7. log the rationale and evidence used.

## Core invariant

`The system may detect influence; it must not secretly become the influencer.`
