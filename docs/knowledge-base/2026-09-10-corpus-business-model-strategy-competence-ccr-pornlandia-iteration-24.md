## 1. Business model ontology
Drzewiecki presents business model and strategy as distinct but complementary concepts. The business model has a broader, more complex morphology including value-chain position, resources, competencies, partners and relationships beyond the firm boundary; strategy emphasizes action, competitive behavior and adaptation. The article also describes both as iterative and suitable for planning, implementation, verification and improvement.

Brzóska describes a business model as a structured set of elements and relations representing a business idea, while strategy expresses actions and behaviors under changing conditions. The model is linked to resources, value creation, value capture, profitability and competitive advantage.

Kardas surveys multiple definitions and typologies. The literature spans financial, operational and strategic views, with recurring themes of value creation/delivery/capture, customer orientation, activities, resources, partners and sustainable advantage. The chapter also notes unresolved definitional and empirical problems.

Oliński separates long-term strategic goals from the value orientation of the business model, while acknowledging substantial overlap in value capture, resources and competencies.

## 2. Business-model dynamics
The corpus rejects a purely static treatment. Business-model elements interact over time; market dynamics can force changes in components and their relationships. Model coherence is therefore an internal design property, not evidence that the model will succeed externally.

## 3. Resource and competence advantage
Siwak treats key organizational competencies as strategic intangible assets that can be rare, difficult to substitute and difficult to imitate. They form strategic potential and should be mapped to functions of strategy. The material also emphasizes the difficulty of directly valuing intangible competencies and the need to connect knowledge management, alliances and international activity with strategic execution.

## 4. Adaptive strategy
The marketing/strategy source distinguishes classical, adaptive, shaping, visionary and survival approaches according to environmental predictability, organizational influence and resource constraints. It also extends Ansoff-style thinking to virtual products and virtual markets and emphasizes hybrid offline/online/social strategies.

## 5. Performance measurement
The corpus warns against evaluating strategy only through short-term sales or financial outcomes. Customer satisfaction, loyalty, operational quality, customer/brand/project value and longer-term strategic capability should also be measured. Observed KPI movement is not by itself causal proof.
