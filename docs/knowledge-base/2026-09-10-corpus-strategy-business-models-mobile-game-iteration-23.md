## 1. Mobile-game source: reusable product architecture
The mobile-game report proposes `Szept Wyroczni / Whisper of Fate`, a voice-controlled social-looter/audio-RPG concept. Its core loop is: voice ritual → AI/RNG verdict → voiced revelation → sanctuary expansion → PvP/return loop. The report positions voice/audio and AI companion interaction as the differentiating layer and describes Unity mobile implementation with local AI, IAP and notifications.

Reusable engineering/product patterns:
- voice-first interaction instead of a conventional tap-only spin;
- AI-mediated dialogue and contextual responses;
- short core loop plus meta progression;
- social competition/co-op/event structures;
- measurable IAP, subscription and ad surfaces;
- Unity mobile delivery and local inference as candidate implementation options;
- telemetry-driven iteration rather than assuming launch success.

The source also contains deliberately manipulative mechanisms: emotional monetization, tone-dependent near misses, approval-linked payment effects and hidden/viral listening ideas. These are retained only as cognitive-security/game-design evaluation fixtures. They are not adopted as requirements for targeting, coercion, addiction engineering, covert monitoring or discriminatory monetization.

## 2. Business-model fundamentals
The PARP `Modele biznesowe dla MŚP` material defines the business model through three questions: how value is created, how it is delivered to the customer, and how the company earns from that value. It explicitly separates business model from strategy and operations and structures the material around Business Model Canvas, business-model mechanisms, competitive advantage, value proposition, Design Thinking, business-model change, analysis and experimentation.

Important portfolio translation:
- every product concept requires an explicit value-creation/delivery/capture model;
- customer segments, channels, relationships and revenue logic must be treated as testable design variables;
- the model should be evaluated and experimentally changed rather than frozen at ideation.

## 3. Strategy vs business model
Wiśniewski/Raczyńska distinguish strategy and business model while emphasizing their complementarity. The business model centers on generating and capturing value; strategy centers more strongly on competitive position. Their conclusion recommends a sequence: develop the business model, prepare and implement strategy, then evaluate and adapt.

Wierzbiński similarly frames strategy as long-term goals and the business model as the enterprise architecture that enables those goals. Other source material notes that business models can include resources, partners and value-chain relationships and may evolve incrementally through organizational learning.

Portfolio invariant:
`business model ≠ strategy ≠ operations`, but the three must be coherently coupled.

## 4. Strategic experimentation and simple rules
Obłój's `Strategia sukcesu firmy` identifies a lifecycle pattern: simple rules for growing firms, improvement for mature firms, and periodic innovation/renewal when routine becomes constraining. The simple-rule set includes clear challenges, strict cost control, ruthless experimentation, focus on important customers, use of employee potential and remembering serious failures as learning experiences.

For the portfolio this becomes an explicit operating loop:
`challenge → hypothesis → bounded experiment → measure → kill/iterate/scale → record learning`.

A large strategic ambition does not remove the requirement for cost discipline or evidence.

## 5. Strategy as adaptive system
`publikacja_PODR_8` presents strategy through evolving schools including plan, pattern, position, resources/capabilities, innovation/new business models and later considerations such as ethics/social responsibility and global strategy. It emphasizes realness, flexibility, communicability and stimulation, and presents strategy as process + content + context anchored in organizational purpose.

The small-firm strategy material describes strategy as both intended and emergent, with the competitive position and resource/capability base changing over time.

Portfolio translation:
- strategy state must be versioned;
- assumptions must be distinguishable from observed outcomes;
- adaptation triggers must be explicit;
- strategic intent must not be confused with achieved performance.

## 6. Strategy concretization and microfoundations
Niewiadomski's work focuses on the level of implementation/concretization of strategy and the microfoundations of the adopted business model. It stresses the gap between abstract strategic formulations and concrete organizational implementation under changing conditions.

This reinforces the portfolio requirement that a strategy artifact must compile into concrete decisions, responsibilities, resources, measures and verification criteria rather than remaining narrative.

## 7. Innovation and differentiation
Kachniewska's study shows how an independent hotel can defend itself against large networks by identifying unique customer needs, redesigning the value proposition and occupying a focused niche. The model-business concept is presented as a long-term way of creating value and adapting to environmental change.

Generalizable pattern:
`market change → identify underserved need → redesign value proposition → configure resources/partners → test viability → build differentiated position`.

This is directly applicable to mobile-game product positioning: differentiation should be based on a genuinely valuable experience, not merely on copying a proven monetization loop.

## 9. Performance and controlling
The supplied `Zarządzanie kosztami i dokonaniami` volume contains a broad performance-management corpus, including balanced scorecards, performance measurement, controlling, cost accounting, R&D project selection, customer-relationship measurement and Wierzbiński's business-model/strategy article.

Portfolio translation:
- product strategy needs measurable objectives;
- financial and non-financial indicators should be separated but jointly interpreted;
- experiments require a baseline and explicit success/failure criteria;
- cost structure is a first-class design variable.
